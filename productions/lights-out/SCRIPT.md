# "The Town Where the Lights Go Out at 9" – Script v1

**Words:** 416 · **Target:** about 116 s at 215 wpm · vertical 9:16 · narrator: Leo (first person)

**Formula:** strange town rule → kid's mocked habit → grandparent → real danger at night → the habit saves the day → last line flips the rule.

|---|---|---|
| **HOOK** |||
| 1 | In my town, the power shuts off every night at exactly nine o'clock. | Night town from above: every window clicks off at once, 9:00 on the clock tower |
| 2 | Not one streetlight. Not one TV. Total darkness. | Dark street, a kid's TV screen dies, total black |
| 3 | So every kid here learns to walk home in the dark by counting steps. | Kids walking home in the dark, lips moving, counting |
| **SETUP** |||
| 4 | Most kids only count the big ones. Three hundred to the bakery. Six hundred to school. | Footsteps; '300' bakery sign, '600' school gate |
| 5 | But I count everything. Stairs. Fence posts. Cracks in the sidewalk. Out loud. | Leo counting a staircase, fence posts and sidewalk cracks, out loud |
| 6 | That's why everyone at school calls me Calculator. | Classmates laughing; 'CALCULATOR' written on his locker |
| 7 | Only one light is allowed to stay on after nine: the lighthouse on the rocks. It has its own generator, and my grandpa has kept it shining for forty years. | The lighthouse on the rocks, its beam the only light; Grandpa wiping the big lens |
| 8 | But grandpa's eyes are getting worse. Now he holds the rail with both hands. | Grandpa's cloudy eyes; both hands gripping the rail |
| 9 | So every evening, I climb with him. Two hundred and fourteen steps to the top. I've counted them a thousand times. | Leo and Grandpa climbing the spiral stairs; '214' at the top |
| 10 | Up there, he showed me an old oil lamp. "If the generator ever dies," he said, "this is what keeps the boats safe." | Close-up: the old brass oil lamp; Grandpa's serious face |
| 11 | And when I counted, grandpa just smiled. "Lighthouse keepers count too." | Grandpa smiling, hand on Leo's shoulder |
| **THE STORM** |||
| 12 | Then came the night of the big storm. | Black clouds, huge waves, rain hammering windows |
| 13 | My dad's fishing boat was still out. He's always home before nine. That night, he wasn't. | Empty harbor; clock 8:55; Mum staring out the window |
| 14 | At nine o'clock, the town went black, like always. | Whole town goes black, one beat of silence |
| 15 | Then, out on the rocks, the lighthouse flickered… and went out. | The lighthouse beam flickers… and dies |
| 16 | The generator was dead. And somewhere in that storm, my dad was heading straight for the rocks, with nothing to guide him. | Dad's small boat in giant waves, black rocks ahead |
| 17 | Grandpa grabbed his coat, but he couldn't even find the door. "Leo," he whispered. "I can't do the stairs." | Grandpa feeling for the door in the dark; close-up whisper |
| **THE CLIMB** |||
| 18 | So I went. | Leo grabs his coat, runs out |
| 19 | Six hundred and twelve steps from our door to the lighthouse. I counted every one in the rain. | Leo running through rain, lips counting; lightning flashes |
| 20 | Inside, it was so dark I couldn't see my own hands. But I didn't need to see. | Inside the lighthouse: pure black, only Leo's breathing |
| 21 | Seventy-one, the cracked step. One hundred and fifty, the turn. Two hundred and fourteen. | In darkness, Leo's hand on the rail; step numbers flash: 71, 150, 214 |
| 22 | I found the matches exactly where grandpa keeps them. Third shelf. Left side. | Hand finds the matchbox: third shelf, left side |
| **THE LIGHT** |||
| 23 | The first match blew out. So did the second. | Match blown out by wind, second one too |
| 24 | The third one caught. The old lamp roared to life, and the giant lens threw its light across the sea. | Third match catches; the lamp roars; the beam sweeps the sea |
| 25 | Far out in the waves, a tiny boat turned away from the rocks. Just in time. | Dad's boat turning hard, missing the rocks by a whisker |
| **ENDING** |||
